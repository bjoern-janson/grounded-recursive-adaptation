# Relationship to Existing Fields

## Overview

Grounded Recursive Adaptation does not replace existing theories of intelligence, learning, control, or alignment.

It addresses a different question:

\[
\boxed{
\text{How does a self-modifying system remain connected to the process that tells it whether it is improving?}
}
\]

Existing fields often study:

- optimization
- representation
- learning
- control
- objectives
- feedback

GRA studies the preservation of the **correction relationship** across recursive transformation.

---

# 1. GRA and AI Alignment

## Traditional Alignment

The classical framing:

\[
\boxed{
\text{How do we give a system the correct objective?}
}
\]

The assumption:

\[
\text{objective}
\rightarrow
\text{optimization}
\rightarrow
\text{desired outcome}
\]

The problem:

- objectives may be incomplete
- proxies may diverge
- self-modification may preserve symbols while losing meaning

---

## GRA Framing

The question becomes:

\[
\boxed{
\text{How do we preserve the ability for reality to correct the system after it changes itself?}
}
\]

The focus shifts from:

\[
\text{value preservation}
\]

to:

\[
\text{correction-channel preservation}
\]

---

# 2. GRA and Reinforcement Learning

## Reinforcement Learning

RL studies:

\[
\boxed{
\text{learning policies from reward signals}
}
\]

Core loop:

\[
S_t,A_t,R_t\rightarrow S_{t+1}
\]

---

## GRA Extension

GRA asks whether the reward relationship itself remains grounded.

A system may optimize:

\[
R_{proxy}
\]

while losing:

\[
R_{real}
\]

The critical distinction:

\[
\boxed{
\text{reward optimization}
\neq
\text{preserved consequence authority}
}
\]

\[

---

The environment retains:

\[
\boxed{

authority.

\[
\Theta\rightarrow0
